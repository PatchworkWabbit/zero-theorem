# Contributing to Zero Theorem

Thank you for your interest in Zero Theorem!

Zero Theorem is an experimental mathematical and computational project exploring emergent information, interconnected operations, and mathematical pathways.

Contributions of all experience levels are welcome. You don't need to be a mathematician or an experienced programmer to participate.

## 1. Explore Freely

You're welcome to:

- Run the engine and investigate its behaviour.
- Experiment with different inputs, operations, and pathways.
- Explore unexpected results or mathematical patterns.
- Suggest new operations, extensions, or research directions.
- Improve performance, documentation, testing, or usability.
- Share questions, observations, and discoveries.

Curiosity is encouraged. Not every experiment needs to produce a useful result.

## 2. Share What You Find

If you discover something interesting, consider opening a GitHub Issue or Discussion.

It helps to include:

- What you were exploring.
- What you observed.
- How someone else might reproduce it, if possible.
- Any code, results, or ideas you'd like to share.

These are suggestions, not requirements. Early ideas and incomplete experiments are welcome too.

## 3. Foundations and Experiments

Zero Theorem has an established foundational model.

New ideas and experimental extensions are encouraged, including approaches that differ from the current implementation.

Where possible, keep experimental changes distinguishable from changes to the established foundation. This helps preserve earlier results and makes different approaches easier to compare.

Proposals to change the foundation can be discussed openly rather than silently replacing its existing rules.

## 4. Code Contributions

Bug fixes, optimisations, new modules, tests, and other improvements are welcome.

For larger changes, opening an Issue first can help contributors coordinate, but it's not required.

Pull requests are welcome, and experimental work doesn't need to be perfect before it's shared.

## 5. Discoveries and Mathematical Claims

Interesting results, unusual patterns, failed experiments, and unanswered questions are all valuable contributions.

Please distinguish between observations, hypotheses, computational evidence, and mathematical proofs when describing findings.

Independent testing and constructive discussion are encouraged.

## 6. Be Respectful

Treat contributors and their ideas with respect.

Questions, disagreement, corrections, and unconventional approaches are welcome. Personal attacks and harassment are not.
